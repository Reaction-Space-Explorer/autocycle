How many enumerated core pairs carry the structure a bistable pair needs.

Ivan and co-workers build bistability from R + X -> 2X, R + Y -> 2Y, X + Y -> P
and X <-> Y: two cycles on one resource, an annihilation consuming a species of
each and returning neither, and a weak interconversion. Sakref and Rivoire, and
Gagrani and co-workers, likewise begin from a pair taken as given. Each condition
is stoichiometric, so each can be counted against the census.

  network               cores  states       pairs    disjoint   competing   exclusive     motif
  glucose G5            1,024     277      38,226      26,003         297         295        69
  glucose+ammonia G4    2,145     416      86,320      67,761       1,472         928        50
  formose G6            2,050     769     295,296     211,651       7,839       1,393       180
  formose+ammonia G4   10,925     810     327,645     259,573      29,837      26,564     3,297
  pyruvic acid G6         861     309      47,586      35,999       1,751         560        72

Three species throughout, at each network's deepest generation. The cores column
sums to 17,005, which is Table 1's total, so the screen runs over the whole census.

states is the number of distinct core species sets, which is not Table 1's
distinct column: that counts distinct stoichiometry, a finer cut, and is the
larger of the two.

Each column adds one condition to the one before it. disjoint is pairs of
compositions sharing no species, which is what it takes to be alternative states.
competing adds a common input, meaning a species both draw on and neither is made
of. exclusive adds a reaction consuming a species of each and returning neither.
motif adds interconversion in both directions, counted strictly as a reaction with
one non-food reactant, since a reaction needing a second substrate is a different
process however it moves the species.

Competition is the condition that bites, and it is not free. Most cores consume no
food at all: 926 of the 1,024 in glucose G5, which leaves 253 of its 277
compositions drawing on no food species, and 25,814 of its 26,003 disjoint pairs
sharing none. The resource two cores compete for is an intermediate the network
makes, not a member of the food set, and it has to be looked for. Asserting the
condition instead of testing it turns 297 competing pairs into 26,003.

What survives is rare and not empty: between 0.07% and 1.3% of disjoint pairs, 50
to 3,297 per network, 3,668 in all. Formose with ammonia is the outlier at every
stage, 11.5% of its disjoint pairs competing against 1.1% for glucose.

This says nothing about whether any pair is bistable. That needs rate constants.
A core is necessary and not sufficient, and a pair of them is no more.

Run on bizon, 16 workers, from papers/cores/scripts/bistable_pairs.py. glucose G5,
formose G6 and pyruvic acid G6 were also run on a second machine and agree row for
row.
