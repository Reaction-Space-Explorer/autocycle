# How many autocatalytic cores are there? It depends what you count

Three-species cores, water and formaldehyde excluded as core members.

| level | what it counts | glucose G5 | formose G6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| cores | every cycle that passes the test | 1,042 | 2,191 |
| distinct stoichiometry | reactions collapsed when identical | 293 | 1,001 |
| formula motifs | steps by formula change and species exchanged | 75 | 144 |
| **mechanisms** | steps by carbon bookkeeping alone | **26** | **44** |

The first row is what a naive enumeration reports and it is inflated twice over:
by rule aliasing, which MOD's own hyperedge count confirms is 15-21% of the
reaction table, and by substrate variation, which is the bulk of the rest. The
same aldol addition of formaldehyde onto a triose and onto a hexose is one
mechanism, not two.

## The mechanism sets are comparable and mostly shared

Glucose 26, formose 44, **19 in common**, and the most frequent mechanism is the
same in both networks: a C6 species adds to the ring, which then sheds a C2 and a
C4. Glucose's mechanisms are almost a subset of formose's; formose carries 25
that glucose does not.

## The extra formose mechanisms are where the chemistry gets doubtful

The largest formose-only mechanism, 140 instances, is
`C+0|in:C0,C1|out:C1  C+6|in:C6|out:-  C-6|in:-|out:C6`: a step that changes the
ring's carbon count by nothing while taking in water and a one-carbon species and
releasing another. That is the methanol shuttle, examined by hand: methanol adds
to an enal, leaves from the isomerised adduct, and a crossed Cannizzaro makes the
second copy. Stoichiometrically sound, and chemically implausible in an aqueous
formaldehyde soup where water outcompetes methanol as a nucleophile by orders of
magnitude, with a Cannizzaro that needs strong base.

So the richer mechanism set of the formose network is partly real and partly an
artefact of a rule set that does not know about concentrations. Quantifying that
split is the paper.
