# How much of the autocatalysis is real chemistry?

Three-species cores, triaged by what each step needs to run. The verdicts are a
stated rule in `src/triage.py`, not a hand list, so they can be re-derived and
argued with.

| | mechanisms | cores | share |
|---|---|---|---|
| **glucose G5** | 26 | 1,042 | |
| serious | 18 | 972 | 93.3% |
| conditional | 7 | 68 | 6.5% |
| artefact | 1 | 2 | 0.2% |
| **formose G6** | 44 | 2,191 | |
| serious | 23 | 1,558 | 71.1% |
| conditional | 9 | 223 | 10.2% |
| artefact | 12 | 410 | **18.7%** |

## The finding

Formose's richer mechanism set is largely artefact. Glucose carries 26
mechanisms of which one is an artefact; formose carries 44 of which twelve are.
Strip them and the gap falls from eighteen mechanisms to seven.

Nearly all of the formose artefact is one thing: **403 of the 410 artefact cores
are the methanol shuttle**, in which methanol adds to an enal, leaves from the
isomerised adduct, and a crossed Cannizzaro supplies the second copy. It is
stoichiometrically sound and chemically not serious: methanol is a poor
nucleophile competing with water in an aqueous formaldehyde soup, and the
Cannizzaro that regenerates it needs strong base. The rule set cannot see this
because it has no concentrations.

The conditional class is smaller and more defensible: Cannizzaro chemistry, which
is real under alkaline conditions, and conjugate additions, which compete with
water but are not absurd. Reported separately rather than merged with either
side.

## What this licenses

Stoichiometric enumeration gives an upper bound on autocatalytic capacity. In
glucose degradation that bound is nearly tight, 93% of it ordinary sugar
chemistry. In the formose network it is loose by a fifth, and the looseness is
one identifiable mechanism rather than diffuse noise. The distance between the
bound and the defensible count, and the fact that it can be attributed to a named
mechanism, is the result.
